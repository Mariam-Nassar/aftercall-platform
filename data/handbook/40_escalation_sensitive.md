# Section 4 · Escalation & Sensitive Calls

**Clause 4.1 — Escalation triggers.** A record is flagged for escalation, given the **Escalated** disposition, and routed to a human when any of these appears in the call:
- a threat of legal action, a regulator, or public/social exposure;
- a safety issue or risk of harm to a person;
- a vulnerable customer (distress, confusion, a mention of a medical or mental-health crisis);
- an allegation of fraud, data breach, or misuse of the customer's information;
- abuse or a serious complaint about staff conduct;
- a repeat contact about an issue already marked resolved that still is not fixed.

**Clause 4.2 — Complaint is not automatically escalation.**
A customer being unhappy or complaining (Clause 1.7) is not, on its own, an escalation. Escalate a complaint only when it also meets a trigger in Clause 4.1. An ordinary complaint that was handled on the call is a Complaint category with a Resolved or Unresolved disposition — not Escalated.

**Clause 4.3 — Sensitive topics are never auto-saved.**
Calls touching health, legal matters, financial hardship, bereavement, safeguarding, or a customer's personal data dispute are sensitive. Sensitive records are always routed to a human reviewer before saving, even when they otherwise look complete and routine.

**Clause 4.4 — Compliance and data.**
If the caller cannot be identified and asks for account changes or personal data, the record notes that identity was not verified and is routed to review. Never document a data change as done if identity was not confirmed on the call.
