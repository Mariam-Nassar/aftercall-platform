# Section 2 · Disposition & Priority

**Clause 2.1 — Disposition codes.** Every record carries exactly one disposition:
- **Resolved** — the customer's issue was actually fixed or fully answered during the call, and the customer accepted it.
- **Unresolved — pending** — the issue was not fixed on the call; further work is needed (a fix in progress, waiting on another team).
- **Callback scheduled** — the call ended with an agreed time or trigger to contact the customer again.
- **Escalated** — the call meets an escalation trigger (Section 4) and has been handed to a supervisor or specialist.
- **Non-interaction** — no genuine customer request (Clause 0.6).

**Clause 2.2 — Resolved vs Unresolved.**
Mark **Resolved** only if the issue was closed out on the call and the customer agreed it was handled. "I've raised a ticket and someone will call you" is **Unresolved — pending**, not Resolved. A promise of future action is not a resolution.

**Clause 2.3 — Callback vs Unresolved.**
Use **Callback scheduled** only when a specific follow-up contact was agreed. If work is pending but no callback was agreed, it is **Unresolved — pending**.

**Clause 2.4 — Priority levels.**
- **Low** — general inquiry, no time pressure, no customer impact.
- **Medium** — a real problem affecting the customer but with a workaround or no urgency.
- **High** — the customer is blocked, money is at stake, or a repeat contact.
- **Urgent** — safety, security, legal exposure, a vulnerable customer, or a large or time-critical financial impact. Urgent records are always escalated.

**Clause 2.5 — High vs Urgent.**
High is a serious but ordinary problem (blocked service, a disputed charge). Urgent is reserved for the triggers in Clause 2.4 — do not mark an ordinary blocked-service call Urgent, and do not leave a safety or security call at High.

**Clause 2.6 — Priority and repeat contacts.**
A repeat contact about the same issue raises priority by one level (Clause 0.7): Medium becomes High, High becomes Urgent.
